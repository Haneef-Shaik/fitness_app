## 2) How independent gyms operate today

- **Paper registers and diaries are the baseline that software replaces.** Owners describe the switch directly: "now i don't need any diary or register to mention students data" (GoGym4U, 12 Sep 2019). "No need of paper and pen" (GymBook, 17 Aug 2023). "Earlier we were doing it manually" (GymOps, 18 Aug 2025) [USER FEEDBACK, S16/S18/S24].
- **Excel and WhatsApp.** A founder writes that Indian gym owners run "their entire business on WhatsApp and Excel". He also reports a Pune owner who was "undercharging 12 members for six months because his spreadsheet had a hidden formula error" [UNVERIFIED anecdote, S38]. Vendors market against "registers and spreadsheets" [COMPANY CLAIM, S16/S22]. Gymzee sells Excel bulk import [S22].
- **Cash and UPI side by side.** Owners ask for a cash/online split: "please incorporate cash or online in Admission fee section" (GymBook, 7 May 2025) and "filter cash, online payment" (GoGym4U, 20 Jun 2019) [USER FEEDBACK]. DGymbook advertises "GST-compliant billing with UPI payment support" [COMPANY CLAIM, S20]. The reviews also confirm one-time admission fees.
- **Membership structures.** Monthly, quarterly, half-yearly and annual plans, partial payments with outstanding dues, and freezes [COMPANY CLAIM, S18/S22]. Annual plans paid in instalments cause disputes. A member wrote that his gym "change[d] my subscription plan to 7000 year plan to 6 month plan because I am late to pay 2000" (GGMS app, 10 Dec 2023) [USER FEEDBACK, S23]. Couple plans: not found.
- **Members are reached over WhatsApp, not apps.** Vendor member apps barely get installed. GymBook Member has 500+ installs against 50K+ for the owner app and a claimed "1M+ members". DGymbook's member app has 100+ installs against a claimed 4,500+ gyms [FACT/COMPANY CLAIM, S17–S21]. Easy Gym explicitly sells member service "inside WhatsApp with no app store download required" [COMPANY CLAIM, S26].
- **Attendance hardware (IndiaMART, accessed 2 Oct 2026)** [COMPANY CLAIM, S32/S33/S34; S35 dated 1 Jul 2026]:
  - eSSL fingerprint units: K21 Pro ₹4,500; K30 Pro ₹3,650–5,432; X990 ₹8,850–12,500; E9C WiFi ₹15,000.
  - eSSL face readers: AIFace Mars ₹14,499; AI Face Magnum ₹15,000.
  - ZKTeco: MB10 ₹30,640; LX15 / LX50 about ₹3,550 online.
  - EM locks ₹1,200; exit button ₹550.
  - Tripod turnstiles ₹38,500–₹1,35,000.
  - Most budget devices are USB- or PC-bound. Only one reviewed model is cloud-WiFi [S35].
  - Gym-membership software does not come in the box. Gym-software vendors advertise integrations instead [S16/S21/S22], and local integrators bundle device plus software [S31].
  - Realtime and Matrix pricing: not found.
- **Proxy entry is a recognised problem.** GoGym4U markets biometric and QR to "Prevent proxy entry & unauthorized access" [COMPANY CLAIM, S16]. An owner asks, in Hinglish, whether software can drive a "biometric gate lock" (1 Sep 2025) [USER FEEDBACK, S18].
- **Low-end Android and vernacular users.** Repeated crash reports on Oppo and Motorola phones [S16/S23], plus Hindi/Hinglish reviews [S18].
- **Facilities beyond the gym floor.** Deloitte found 50% of facilities offer group classes and 30% offer steam baths. Value gyms commonly give 1–2 weeks of complimentary personal training [FACT-survey, S1].

## 3) Market numbers

**Main source: Deloitte–HFA "India fitness market report 2025" (Sep 2025)** [ESTIMATE – consultancy, but the most rigorous source available. Method: 58,000+ centres web-scraped, 840 in-person visits, about 2,800 phone interviews, 3,004 consumers surveyed. Excludes free public, hotel and corporate gyms] [S1, S2]:

| Metric | 2024 | 2030 (projected) |
|---|---|---|
| Fitness facilities | ~46,500 | 65,500 |
| Members | 12.3M | 23.2–23.3M |
| Penetration of population | 0.8% | 1.7% |
| Revenue | ₹16,200 cr (US$1.94B) | ₹37,700 cr |
| Personal training share of revenue | ₹2,450 cr (14%) | ₹7,200 cr (20%) |

- **Value segment** (<₹8,000 to ₹14,000/yr; 500–3,000 sq ft): 37,200 centres, 9.6M members, ₹9,000 cr. That is 80% of centres, 78% of members and 56% of revenue.
- **Premium segment** (₹14,000 to ₹50,000+/yr): 5,400 centres, 2.2M members, ₹6,100 cr.
- **Boutique segment** (some at ₹19,000–30,000/yr): 3,900 centres, 0.5M members, ₹1,100 cr. Fastest growing at about 19% CAGR [S1].
- **Geography.** The top 10 cities hold about 14,100–14,500 centres (31%), 5.1M members (41–42%) and ₹9,100 cr (56% of revenue). The rest of India holds 32,000 centres, 7.1M members and ₹7,100 cr [S1]. The report prints both 14,100 and 14,500 for top-10 centres.
- **Demand funnel.** 956M Indians are aged 18–62. 138M are active. Only 21M pay for fitness, including 12M at gyms or studios [S1].
- **Barriers and churn.** 52% of non-members cite affordability. 48% of people who quit say they would rejoin. 32% quit as a "temporary break" and 32% switch to home workouts [S1].
- **Derived figures** [ANALYSIS, from S1]:
  - About 265 members per facility on average: value about 258, premium about 407, boutique about 128.
  - Rest-of-India facilities average about 222 members and about ₹22 lakh revenue per year.
  - A value gym averages about ₹24 lakh revenue per year, or about ₹9,400 per member per year.
  - GymBook's claimed 3,000 gyms and 1M members (about 333 per gym) is roughly consistent [S17].
- **Fee bands per month** [ESTIMATE, low reliability; fintech blog, S37, 2 Sep 2026]: basic ₹700–1,200, standard AC ₹1,200–2,000, premium ₹3,000–6,000.
- **Tier-2 gym economics, same source** [ESTIMATE, S37]:
  - Fixed costs for an 1,800 sq ft gym are about ₹2 lakh/month: rent ₹72K, payroll ₹80K, and power/AMC/software/marketing ₹48K.
  - Break-even is about 200 members at a ₹1,000 realised fee.
  - Trainer pay is ₹15–25K for freshers (about ₹22K average) and ₹35–60K for experienced trainers.
  - Metro rent runs ₹40–100 per sq ft per month.
- **Ken Research** [ESTIMATE, S3] rolls Deloitte forward: 49,300 facilities and 13.65M members in 2025, and about US$158 revenue per member in 2024.
- **Cult.fit (DRHP filed 6 Jul 2026)** [FACT as reported, S6/S7/S8]:
  - 708 centres in 77 cities at 31 Mar 2026: 218 company-owned, 288 franchise and **202 independent "marketplace" gyms**.
  - FY26 revenue ₹1,720.6 cr; net loss ₹251.8 cr; 9.87 lakh paid members.
  - Four metros produced 90.44% of services revenue.
  - Renewal is "around 50%" [secondary report, S9]. Cult Neo, its low-price format, charges about ₹10–12K/yr [S9].
- **GST.** From 22 Sep 2025, gym services moved from 18% with input tax credit to a **mandatory 5% without input tax credit** [FACT – CBIC via Business Today, 16 Sep 2025, S4; S5]. Software subscriptions are still sold "+18% GST" [S17].
- **Not found in a credible source:** a split between organized and unorganized gyms beyond Deloitte's value/premium/boutique tiers, seasonality (January or post-Diwali spikes), the trainer/gym split on PT revenue, supplements' share of gym revenue, and post-COVID closure counts.

## 4) Owner pain points with evidence

1. **Rent and an economic model that depends on no-shows.** A Chennai strength-facility owner writes that at the average Indian gym fee, "almost half the revenue would be funneled into rent. 10-15 percent would cover maintenance." He says gyms charge "a fee that factors for only 1 out of 2-3 clients actually showing up" and must "pray no one opens an identical location across from mine" [USER FEEDBACK, S36, 8 Nov 2025].
2. **Collecting fees and dues.** Owners praise software for showing "how many members have paid the fees" (DGymbook, 8 Nov 2023) [USER FEEDBACK, S21]. Vendors lead with dues recovery ("72% Dues reduction") [COMPANY CLAIM, S26].
3. **Software vendor support and reliability.** This is the dominant complaint category across GoGym4U, GymBook, GGMS, Gymzee and the Genius-group app (see table). Some reviews escalate to calling a vendor "fraud" (GymBook, 12 May 2024) [USER FEEDBACK, S18].
4. **Renewal price hikes.** GymBook's renewal went from ₹1,800 to ₹2,700 + GST. GoGym4U went from ₹100 to ₹350 a month [USER FEEDBACK, S16/S18].
5. **Gaps in PT and session-pack workflows.** "no Trainer field in PT details" (GymBook, 17 Jul 2026). "we have to manually count the number of sessions" (GoGym4U, 23 Feb 2023) [USER FEEDBACK].
6. **Supplement retail.** A gym-supplement distributor asked for "stock inventory and billing… i can pay extra amount for that feature" (GoGym4U, 15 Sep 2022) [USER FEEDBACK].
7. **Aggregators.** FITPASS non-payment complaints. A five-branch owner calls support "extremely poor and unresponsive" (28 Jul 2026) [USER FEEDBACK, S29].
8. **Cult.fit competition.** Cult offers co-branding money and demand to independents, and already counts 202 of them as marketplace gyms [COMPANY CLAIM/FACT, S6/S10].
9. **GST.** The 5% rate without input tax credit means GST paid on rent, equipment and software can no longer be offset [FACT, S4]. Equipment still attracts 18% [ESTIMATE, S37].
10. **Trainer attrition, freeloading and Reddit/Quora evidence: not found** in fetchable sources. Reddit and Economic Times were blocked during this run.

## 5) Willingness to pay and switching

- **Price anchors cluster around ₹2,000–₹12,000 per year for an independent gym** [COMPANY CLAIM, S14–S25]:
  - GoGym4U ₹2,499/yr; GymBook ₹2,199–2,599 + GST; DGymbook about ₹6,000/yr; Okfit ₹6,000–36,000/yr; GGMS ₹11,999/yr; GymOwl ₹17,999/yr.
  - Chain-grade tools: FitnessForce from ₹1,500/month; Wellyx and GymMaster about $89–299/month.
  - Local one-time licences: ₹10K–₹1.6L.
- **Relative to revenue** [ANALYSIS]: ₹2.5–12K is about 0.1–0.5% of a typical value gym's ~₹24 lakh annual revenue (from S1). Free tiers and trials already exist: GoGym4U's 25-member free tier, Okfit's 7-day trial [S16/S25].
- **Price sensitivity is still high.** Complaints about ₹100→₹350 a month and ₹1,800→₹2,700 a year show that a few hundred rupees triggers anger [USER FEEDBACK].
- **Owners will pay for specific modules:** inventory [S16], and class calendars or booking ("looking and willing to pay for an app…", 3 Jul 2019, S16) [USER FEEDBACK].
- **Why they switch:**
  - Away from poor support or bugs: "Gogym is better than ggms , west of time and west of money" [S23].
  - After trying several apps: "I have tried many apps… didn't find any app as good as GymBook" [S18]. "I tried desktop support software… also tried other Indian gym management apps" [S16].
  - Excel bulk import lowers switching cost [S22].
- **Survey-based willingness-to-pay data for Indian owners: not found.**

## 6) Commoditized features vs underserved gaps (ANALYSIS)

**Commoditized** (in nearly every product at ₹200–1,000 a month): member records, plan expiry and renewals, dues, GST invoices, WhatsApp/SMS reminders, QR attendance, biometric integration, static diet and workout PDFs, staff roles, enquiry tracking and basic reports. Because cheap and free options are everywhere, "free software" on its own is a weak wedge. The real switching barriers are trust and support quality.

**Underserved:**
1. **Member engagement that members actually use.** Vendor member apps get 100–500 installs, and vendors are retreating to WhatsApp [S19/S21/S26]. That cuts both ways for the B2B2C plan: it is evidence of a gap, and a warning that members resist installing an app their gym hands them. The member app has to be valuable on its own merits.
2. **Real progress tracking for members:** logging, personal records, measurements and Indian-food nutrition, rather than PDF plans. The Deloitte retention problem (32% "temporary breaks") suggests a market for owner-facing churn signals built from attendance and progress data [S1].
3. **PT session and trainer attribution, and session-pack counting** [S16/S18].
4. **Supplement and store inventory** [S16/S22].
5. **Reliable, responsive support in Hindi and Hinglish on low-end Android phones.** This is the most-cited failure in reviews.
6. **Aggregator payout reconciliation** for FITPASS and cultpass PRO gyms [S29].
7. **Owner trust.** Cult.fit already bundles demand plus CRM for independents [S10]. A free tool whose business model depends on the gym's members will need explicit data-ownership and non-poaching assurances. No direct evidence was found either way on this; it is an inference from the aggregator complaints.
