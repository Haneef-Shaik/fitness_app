## 2) Business models: where the money actually comes from

- **Three revenue layers.**
  - A SaaS fee charged per location (Mindbody, GymMaster), per active member (Zen Planner, TeamUp) or per client seat (Trainerize, Hevy Coach, TrueCoach).
  - A take rate on payments.
  - Add-ons: branded app $39–$99/month (S35, S37, S26), marketing CRM, AI.
- **Payments are the hidden core.** [FACT/COMPANY CLAIM] ABC processes $8.8B+ a year (S20), Xplor $47B (S44) and ClubRight £182M (S39).
  - [ANALYSIS] For a gym with $15k/month in card volume, PushPress Free costs about $749 in processing. Pro costs about $434 in processing plus $159, i.e. about $593. So "free" costs roughly $156/month more at that volume.
- **Marketplace layer:** Mindbody's capped first-booking fee (S5) and ClassPass's margin between what members pay and what studios receive (S11).
- **Corporate B2B2C:** in Wellpass the employer pays and the gym gets members (S2, S14).
- **Equipment pull-through:** EGYM and Technogym use software to sell and lock in hardware (S15, S46).
- **India-relevant [FACT via Wikipedia, citing Business Standard 16 Sep 2026]:**
  - UPI person-to-merchant payments up to ₹2,000 carry no merchant fee.
  - From 15 Oct 2026, a 0.4% fee (capped at ₹300) applies above ₹2,000.
  - Small merchants under the "P2PM" framework are exempt, and merchants may not pass the fee on to customers (S51).
  - [ANALYSIS] The 3–5% card take rate that pays for "free" US gym software has no equivalent on India's UPI rails. A free gym product in India has to be funded on the consumer side.

## 5) Retention science

**The "50% quit within 6 months" figure: its real origin**
- [FACT] It comes from 1980s exercise-adherence research on *supervised exercise programmes*, not from gym membership data.
- Dishman's 1986 abstract: "Despite 15 years of research on exercise compliance, supervised exercise programs typically have dropout rates of 50%" (S62).
- A 1995 review states: "Dishman (1982) stated that 50% of the individuals who begin an exercise programme discontinue it within 6 months" (S63). The 1982 primary paper was not retrieved [UNVERIFIED].
- [UNVERIFIED] Industry blogs attribute the figure to IHRSA/HFA; no HFA primary source was found (S64).
- [ANALYSIS] The figure is directionally plausible but it is not a measured gym churn statistic.

**Measured gym data**
- [FACT] Sperandei et al., 2016, J Sci Med Sport: 5,240 members of a Rio de Janeiro gym, 2005–2014. "63% of new members will abandon activities before the third month, and less than 4% will remain for more than 12 months of continuous activity." This measures continuous attendance, not paid membership (S65).
- [FACT] Sperandei, 2019: 38% of people who drop out return within 12 months, and more than half of those return in the first month. This is a win-back window (S66).
- [FACT] Bedford, 2013: about 350,000 members at 100 UK sites over 4 years. "Only 52 per cent of members are maintaining membership for 12 months" (S67, 4 Sep 2013).
- [FACT] ukactive/Mazars, 2015, 600+ UK sites: average stay was 11.2 months on 12-month contracts vs 17.2 months on flexible contracts, and 23.5 months private vs 13.2 months public (S68).
  - Bedford, on data from 4,500+ facilities, says the opposite: month-to-month members have the shortest retention (S70). The evidence conflicts.
- [FACT, via Keepme] IHRSA 2017 Profiles of Success: average annual attrition of 28.6%. This is club-reported and subject to survivorship bias (S69).

**What predicts retention**
- **Visits in the first month.** [FACT, expert-reported; dataset not public] Bedford, citing Hillsdon 2002:
  - Members are "much more likely to stay customers if they visit a minimum of once per week or four times per month in the first month".
  - "people who come four times a month stay at least 13 weeks longer".
  - Members visiting 8–12 times a month stay "about 15 months extra" compared with once a week (S70).
- **Staff interaction.**
  - [FACT] Bedford: one staff interaction in a month raises the chance the member is still using the club next month by 20%; 2–3 interactions by 50%; 4 or more by 80% (S70).
  - [FACT] IHRSA/TRP 2016 (13,000+ UK members): at-risk members who had a successful re-engagement interaction were "45 per cent less likely to cancel". Any interaction reduced dropout by nearly 10% (S71, 22 Jul 2016).
- **Social ties.** [FACT] TRP/Hillsdon survey of 10,062 members at about 200 clubs (2013):
  - Members who made friends were 40% less likely to cancel.
  - Gym-floor-only members were 56% more likely to cancel than class-goers.
  - Regular staff contact plus a friend meant 53% lower risk.
  - Retention was over 90% with regular face-to-face staff contact vs 80% without (S72).
- **Group exercise.** [COMPANY CLAIM; correlational] Les Mills/TRP across 601 facilities: average stay of 35.3 months for members attending 3+ times a week vs 23.4 months for those attending none (S73).
- **Who drops out.** [FACT] Age, prior activity level, BMI and motivations (weight loss, hypertrophy, health, aesthetics) all shift the risk of quitting (S65). Enjoyment and reaching one's own goals matter too (S72).
- **Digital nudges.** [COMPANY CLAIM] Bedford's non-sales messages sent after visits 1–4 raised Lifestyle Fitness visit frequency from 1.5 to 2.5 a week (S70).

## 7) Commoditised features vs underserved gaps [ANALYSIS]

**Commoditised (expected for free or near-free):**
- scheduling and booking
- recurring billing
- QR or PIN check-in
- waivers
- POS
- basic CRM, email and SMS
- reporting
- a branded app shell (now a $39–$99 add-on, or included free in PushPress)
- AI chat or front desk, which is fast becoming table stakes

**Underserved:**
1. **Member training logging inside gym apps.** Gym apps are door keys: workouts are about 3.5% of check-ins in ABC's data (S22). AF users ask for custom workout logging and nutrition (S58), and EGYM users ask for access to their own programmes (S17). Hevy shows a consumer-grade logger can be the hook (S49).
2. **Nutrition.** Present only in some products (Virtuagym, Trainerize, ABC Ignite's 16M meals). AI food-photo tracking was not found in any profiled gym vendor's member app; this is absence of evidence, not proof it doesn't exist.
3. **A portable member identity.** Single-gym branded apps have no network effects. A consumer app that follows members from gym to gym (the Hevy model) does. But Mindbody's Universal Login episode (S7, unverified) shows owners revolt when the platform appears to take their customer.
4. **Retention tooling built on the science.** Prompts to reach 4+ visits in the first month, staff-interaction task lists, buddy and friend matching, nudges toward classes, and an early win-back window. Vendors mostly report attendance and send emails (S70–S72).
5. **Emerging-market economics.** Per-location USD pricing paid for by card take rates does not carry over to UPI-heavy India (S51).

**Implications for "free gym software as a distribution channel":**
- **Free software has precedents, but each was monetised another way.** PushPress Free (payments), ClubRight's 3 months free and Trainerize's free tier (upsell). In India the consumer side must carry the revenue.
- **The app must earn the install.** Own check-in, billing and booking so members install it, as Planet Fitness's adoption shows (S53), then retain them with logging and nutrition.
- **Avoid channel conflict.**
  - Never charge commission on a gym's own members.
  - Never show members rival gyms.
  - Guarantee free data export, given the export-fee complaints (S7, S24).
  - Make the gym the brand the member sees in the app.
- **Give owners a retention dashboard** (first-month visit gaps, members at risk) as the reason to stay on the platform.
