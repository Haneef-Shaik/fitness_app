## 2) Strava: the network-effect benchmark

**Scale:**
- 180M+ users in 185+ countries [COMPANY CLAIM, S37].
- About 50M monthly active users and downloads up 80% year on year in 2025, roughly double the nearest competitor [ESTIMATE (Sensor Tower), S38].
- Users open the app 35+ times a month, against fewer than 15 for competitors [ESTIMATE (Sensor Tower, Sep 2023), S39].

**Money:**
- **Subscription revenue.** Sensor Tower estimated $180M+ in in-app subscription revenue for Jan–Sep 2025 [ESTIMATE, S38]. This counts only app-store purchases.
- **Company-stated recurring revenue.** Annual recurring revenue "approaching ~$500M", stated by the company in Aug 2025 as reported second-hand [COMPANY CLAIM, S40].
- **Third-party estimates.** Two estimates put 2025 revenue at $415–490M [ESTIMATE, S40].
- **Valuation.** $2.2B including debt in May 2025 [FACT (reported), S38].
- **IPO status.**
  - A confidential draft S-1 was reported on 8 Jan 2026 and confirmed on 2 Feb 2026.
  - As of 15 Aug 2026 there was no public S-1 and no pricing [FACT (secondary source), S40].
  - Former Spotify/Netflix CFO Barry McCarthy joined the board [FACT, S40].
- **Paying subscribers:** never disclosed [S40].

**Year in Sport 2025** (published 2–3 Dec 2025) [COMPANY CLAIM, S37]:
- 14 billion kudos given in the year.
- 1 million clubs in total. New clubs nearly quadrupled; running clubs grew 3.5× and club events 1.5×.
- Lifting is growing on Strava itself:
  - Gen Z is twice as likely as Gen X to call weight training their main sport.
  - Women were 21% more likely than men to record a Weight Training activity.
- The CEO says over half of Gen Z plan to use Strava more in 2026.

**Monetising the network.** In May 2020 Strava moved previously free features behind the paywall, and it raised prices in early 2023 [FACT, S41].

**The November 2024 API restrictions** [FACT, S42]. Three changes:
- Third-party apps may show a user's Strava data only to that user.
- Strava data may not be used for AI or machine learning.
- Broad bans on "analytics… customer insights".

The changes broke coaching platforms such as Intervals.icu and TrainerRoad and analytics tools such as VeloViewer. Strava's stated reason was "additional safeguards around our community's data" [COMPANY CLAIM, S42].

**Acquisitions:**
- Runna (AI running plans): definitive agreement on 17 Apr 2025 [FACT, S43].
- The Breakaway (cycling training): core assets bought on 22 May 2025 [FACT, S44].

**The Garmin dispute:**
- **Filing.** On 30 Sep 2025 Strava sued Garmin over its segments patent (filed 2011, granted 2015) and heatmaps patent (filed 2014, granted 2016), seeking an injunction.
- **Dismissal.** Strava voluntarily dismissed the suit without prejudice on 21 Oct 2025.
- **Statements.**
  - Strava: "We're glad to put this behind us" [FACT, S45].
  - Strava's CPO, writing on Reddit, linked the suit to Garmin's "new developer guidelines for all API partners" [COMPANY CLAIM, S45].
- **Lesson.** The platform that holds the data (Garmin) has leverage over the platform that holds the social layer.

**Academic support for network effects.** A study of about 1.1M runners covering 350M km found exercise is socially contagious, using weather as a natural experiment. Less-active runners influence more-active ones; men are influenced by both sexes, women only by women [FACT, S46].

**Why running's network effect worked and lifting apps struggle to copy it** [ANALYSIS]:
1. **Comparable units.** A segment is the same stretch of road for everyone, so time ranks strangers objectively. Lifting numbers depend on bodyweight, range of motion and equipment, and they are self-reported. That makes global leaderboards weak outside friends or a single gym.
2. **Effortless content.** GPS devices upload automatically, which keeps the feed full. Lifting needs manual entry, and wearables still fail at it: Garmin's rep counting and weight capture are unreliable, so lifters use the watch as a timer and log in Strong or Hevy [S36].
3. **Place.** Segments, heatmaps and clubs are tied to real places and real-world meetups. A lifter's "segment" is their gym, which global consumer apps don't model.
4. **Feed appeal.** Maps and photos of outdoor sessions make better posts than "Push Day A".
5. **Strava already owns the graph.** It is absorbing lifters (the weight-training data above), and lifting apps feed into it (Hevy's Strava integration). Hevy's social layer works mainly as a distribution channel (share cards, follow loops) rather than a defensible lock-in.

## 3) Nutrition and AI evidence

**MyFitnessPal:**
- **Ownership.** Under Armour bought it for $475M (Feb 2015, 80M users) and sold it to Francisco Partners for $345M (Oct 2020). A 2018 breach exposed 150M accounts [FACT, S47].
- **Size.** 270M+ members [COMPANY CLAIM, Feb 2025, S48].
- **Traffic and revenue.** About 900K downloads a month and about $13M revenue a month [ESTIMATE; the underlying source isn't stated, S49].
- **Barcode paywall.** Barcode scanning became Premium-only on 1 Oct 2022, when Premium was about $20/mo [FACT, S50]. Users reacted strongly: "a decade old free feature moves behind a paywall"; "everyone isn't gonna just download Lose It" [USER FEEDBACK, S50]. The same change appears again in the review quotes in the "Commoditised" list in §7.
- **Acquisitions:**
  - Intent, an AI meal planner, on 12 Feb 2025 [FACT, S48].
  - Cal AI: the deal closed in Dec 2025 and was announced on 2 Mar 2026; Cal AI continues as a separate app [FACT, S51].
- **The value of history.** One reviewer: "they save all your food data from years ago!" [USER FEEDBACK, S52].

**Cal AI:**
- 15M+ downloads and $30M+ annual revenue within two years, with a team of seven [COMPANY CLAIM, via TechCrunch, S51]. Another outlet reports $40M+ [S49].
- Rated 4.8 from 368K ratings [FACT, S53].
- Accuracy complaint: "sodium, fats and at times fiber was way off… if I still have to… recheck all of my meals… it defeats the purpose" [USER FEEDBACK, S53].
- The founder claims about 90% accuracy on visible foods [COMPANY CLAIM, S54].
- Growth was driven by fitness influencers on TikTok (e.g. Alex Eubank) [UNVERIFIED, low reliability, S55].

**MacroFactor:**
- **User growth** [COMPANY CLAIM, S56]:

  | Sep 2022 | Sep 2023 | Sep 2024 | Sep 2025 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 35K | 90K | 185K | 400K |

- **Workouts app.** A companion workouts app went public in Jan 2026 [COMPANY CLAIM, S56].
- **AI logging stance.** It "prioritizes real, lab-analyzed food data" [COMPANY CLAIM, S56].
- **Pricing.** $11.99/mo or $71.99/yr; rated 4.8 from 23K ratings [FACT, S57].
- **Why loyal users love it.** Its expenditure estimate adapts to the user's actual intake and weight trend instead of a formula:
  - A dietitian: "after a day, I stopped MFP because this app is so much better" [USER FEEDBACK, S57].
  - A physician: the algorithm "becomes more accurate over time" [USER FEEDBACK, S57].

**Other nutrition apps:**
- **Cronometer:** 15M users; barcode scanning free; positions itself on "lab-analyzed" data; sells a Pro product to dietitians and clinicians [COMPANY CLAIM, S58].
- **SnapCalorie:** claims "2x more accurate than nutritionists" and 100K+ active users [COMPANY CLAIM, S59].
- **Noom:** cut staff while shifting toward GLP-1 medication programmes ($149 for the first month, then $279/mo) [FACT; date not verified, S60].
- **Lose It:** company figures not found (site blocked).

**How accurate is AI photo calorie estimation?**
- **NIH (NIDDK) study, NUTRITION 2026, July 2026.** 102 meals were weighed to 0.1 g, then photographed into four apps. Every app underestimated calories [FACT, preliminary and not yet peer-reviewed, S61, S62]:

  | App | Calories underestimated per meal |
  |---|---|
  | Appediet | 252 kcal |
  | MyFitnessPal | 327 kcal |
  | Lose It | 333 kcal |
  | Cal AI | 345 kcal |

  - Fat was underestimated by about 30 g, and totals were about one-third too low overall.
  - Errors were worse for high-fat/keto meals.
  - Researcher: "take the results with a grain of salt."
- **Li et al., *Nutrients*, 2024** [FACT, S63]:
  - **Identifying foods** in a photo was good: MyFitnessPal 97%, Fastic 92%, HealthifyMe 90%, Foodvisor 87%.
  - **Calorie estimates** for mixed or culturally diverse dishes ranged from −76% to +270%.
  - **Manual logging** overestimated a Western diet by 1,040 kJ and underestimated an Asian diet by 1,520 kJ (about 363 kcal).
  - **Why this matters for India** [ANALYSIS]: Indian food is mostly mixed dishes (curries, dal, thalis). A step where the user reviews and corrects the AI's estimate is a real advantage, not a gimmick.
- **AI as a commodity.** Industry commentary says "AI multi-modal input across photo, voice, and text is becoming table stakes" [ESTIMATE, S49].

**AI workout plans, and ChatGPT as a competitor:**
- A chatbot's exercise advice was 41.2% comprehensive and 90.7% accurate [FACT, S64].
- ChatGPT's 12-week resistance programmes were reasonable drafts that "require human expertise" [FACT, S65].
- AI-powered subscription apps earn 41% more revenue per payer but churn 30% faster [FACT (RevenueCat dataset), S66].

So, from users' point of view [ANALYSIS]:
- **Seen as useful:** load and rep suggestions grounded in the user's own logged history (Alpha Progression, Fitbod at its best), and "I never have to think" convenience.
- **Seen as gimmicky:** generic plan generators, which ChatGPT replicates for free, and "AI" that feels random (the Fitbod complaints).

**Why users abandon health apps.** A survey of 1,604 US phone owners found 45.7% had stopped using some health apps. The main reasons were "high data entry burden, loss of interest, and hidden costs" [FACT, S69].

**Do social features, competition and gamification improve adherence?**
- **Social features.** A systematic review of 19 studies found no significant effect on physical activity (SMD 0.957, 95% CI −1.09 to 3.00). Preferences were mixed: competition motivates some users and puts others off [FACT, S70].
- **Gamification with social incentives (the STEP UP trial, JAMA Internal Medicine 2019, n=602)** [FACT, S71]:

  | Arm | Extra daily steps vs control, during | During follow-up |
  |---|---|---|
  | Competition | +920 | +569 (the only significant arm) |
  | Support | +689 | not significant |
  | Collaboration | +637 | not significant |

- **Gamification in general.** A meta-analysis of 16 randomised trials (2,407 people) found a small-to-medium effect, shrinking to very small/small about 14 weeks after the intervention ended [FACT, S72].
- **The Duolingo model** [COMPANY CLAIM (ex-CPO), S73]:
  - Raising retention of current users by 21% produced 4.5× daily active users over four years.
  - Streak mechanics included streak-saver notifications and streak freezes.
  - Leaderboards that match users by engagement level rather than friendship raised learning time 17% and tripled the number of highly engaged learners.
  - "Protect the channel": avoid notification fatigue.

## 7) Commoditised vs underserved [ANALYSIS]

**Commoditised** (free or cheap everywhere, so not a differentiator):
- Set/rep logging, PR and e1RM charts, timers.
- Program libraries: Boostcamp gives away 11,000+ for free.
- Barcode scanning: free in Cronometer. When MyFitnessPal paywalled it, users left.
- AI photo logging: "table stakes."
- Share cards, CSV export, Apple Health sync.
- Generic AI plans: ChatGPT already writes passable drafts.

Pricing is anchored low by Hevy's $23.99/yr.

**Underserved:**
1. **AI nutrition that is honest about its accuracy, with a step to review and correct estimates**, especially for mixed and regional cuisines. Every app tested by the NIH underestimated calories by about one-third, and Asian diets carry larger errors.
2. **Strength, nutrition and body-composition tracking in one app.** MacroFactor only reached workouts in Jan 2026; Cal AI and MyFitnessPal don't do lifting; JustGains' founder describes this exact pain.
3. **A social graph at gym level.** The gym is lifting's natural "segment." Hyrox, Orangetheory and CrossFit show that venue-anchored leaderboards and challenges drive community, which no consumer lifting app owns.
4. **Coaching tools that cover nutrition and billing.** Hevy Coach lacks both.
5. **Strength logging that wearables can't do.** Garmin and Apple remain weak, so a fast phone-based logger stays necessary.
6. **Sustained adherence.** Competitive formats outlast purely supportive ones (STEP UP), but every gamification effect fades. Real-world, event-driven loops like the CrossFit Open or Hyrox races beat "social feeds" alone.
